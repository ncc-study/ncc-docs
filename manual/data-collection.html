2  Data collection

How study data is gathered and where it comes from

The NCC-study (Koronastudien) combines self-reported questionnaire data with linked national health-registry data and biological samples. This page describes each of those sources, how participants consent, and how the data reaches the secure processing environment. For how the resulting datasets are structured and joined, see Data management.

2.1 Overview

Data is collected through three channels that are later linked on a single participant key:

  1. Questionnaires — participants answer online questionnaires hosted on Nettskjema, the University of Oslo’s survey platform.
  2. Health registries — national health registries contribute records for consenting participants under the study’s approvals.
  3. Biological samples — biobank and laboratory results (e.g. serology from dried blood spots).

All three are delivered into TSD (Tjenester for Sensitive Data), where they are processed into research-ready datasets. Person-level data never leaves TSD.

Tip

Every source is ultimately keyed to deltaker_id, the study’s unique participant identifier, which is what makes the sources joinable. How that key is assigned is covered under Participant identification.

2.2 Questionnaires

Questionnaires are the core data-collection instrument. Each questionnaire is a Nettskjema form, and every questionnaire is identified by a single-letter family used as the prefix on all of its variables.

R Report R1–R6 CLOC sub-study report questionnaires
E Extra E1, E2, E3 Supplementary instruments — E2 has sub-forms (BRIEF, RAND-12, ISARIC)

2.2.1 How questionnaire data is collected

  • Participants complete questionnaires online in Nettskjema.
  • Each submission is a single response; a participant answers a given questionnaire at most once, which is why the delivered data is naturally wide (one row per participant per form).
  • Nettskjema assigns each submission an internal reference (submission_reference). This is used during processing to identify the participant, but is not stable across re-exports and is not a research variable.
  • The questionnaire item text and response options are the authoritative definition of each variable; they are preserved as variable and value labels in the delivered data.

2.3 Consent

Consent is handled as a separate datastream from the questionnaires, so that participation status can be managed independently of the substantive responses.

  • Consent capture — participants provide informed consent, recorded via Nettskjema.
  • Identification lookups — consent processing maintains lookup tables that map a participant’s Nettskjema submission_reference, phone number, or email to their deltaker_id.
  • Revocation — participants may withdraw at any time. Withdrawn participants are recorded on a revoked-consent list, and their responses are removed during processing.
Important

Consent is not a one-time gate. Revoked consents are re-applied on every processing run, so a withdrawal removes the participant from all subsequently delivered datasets. Analyses are always run against the current consent state.

2.4 From Nettskjema to TSD

Questionnaire and consent data move from Nettskjema into the secure environment automatically:

  1. Participants submit responses in Nettskjema.
  2. The TSD data loader transfers submissions into TSD as raw, semicolon-delimited CSV files (one file per questionnaire form).
  3. Inside TSD, each raw file is processed independently into a cleaned, labelled per-form dataset.

Because TSD has no network access, everything downstream of this transfer — cleaning, linkage, analysis — happens entirely within TSD. No study data is stored or processed outside it.

2.5 Health registry linkage

For consenting participants, national health registries contribute records that extend the self-reported data with objective, longitudinal health information. Registry data is delivered to TSD as approved extracts and linked to participants on the study key.

Registry Full name Contributes
MSIS Meldingssystem for smittsomme sykdommer Notifiable infectious-disease diagnoses (incl. SARS-CoV-2)
SYSVAK Nasjonalt vaksinasjonsregister Vaccinations (incl. COVID-19 doses)
DÅR Dødsårsaksregisteret Cause-of-death records
NPR Norsk pasientregister Hospital / specialist-care contacts
LMR Legemiddelregisteret Dispensed prescription medications
Note

Registry data is often longitudinal (multiple events per participant over time). Before delivery it is summarised to one row per participant so it stays joinable on deltaker_id without inflating row counts. What each registry extract covers (time span, event types) is fixed by the approved data delivery.

Important

Some registry deliveries carry strict handling conditions — for example, a national identity number may be prohibited from being stored alongside the registry records, with linkage performed through a project-specific key only. These conditions are enforced in processing and are why registry data lives in the most restricted storage tier.

2.6 Biological samples

The study also collects biological material and derived laboratory results.

  • Dried blood spots (DBS) and related samples are collected from participants.
  • Laboratory analyses (e.g. serology / antibody measurements) are performed and the results delivered to TSD.
  • Biobank results are linked to participants on deltaker_id and processed the same way as other sources.

2.7 Participant identification

Every source above is linked to a single key, deltaker_id, which is what allows questionnaires, registries, and biobank data to be joined together.

During processing, deltaker_id is assigned by matching each response against the consent lookup tables, in order of reliability:

  1. Nettskjema submission_reference
  2. Phone number
  3. Email address

Once assigned, deltaker_id is the only identifier retained for linkage; direct contact details (phone, email) are dropped from processed data. See Data management for how deltaker_id is used to join datasets.
